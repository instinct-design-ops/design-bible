# Alpha One Labs

Client rules in force for Alpha One Labs, a Roveir (RV agency) client. Read this file together with design-bible.md before any Alpha One work. Where this file and design-bible.md overlap, this file is the more specific one and wins for Alpha One. Replaced versions are in history/superseded-rules.md. The review rounds behind these rules are history/rejected-directions.md entries 4 to 8.

Alpha One Labs is a research-use-only brand, so design-bible.md R-01 to R-03 apply: no humans or hands, empty or 10%-powder vials, blank white labels.

## Brand

- **AO-01** · 21 Nov 2026. Client file: "Alpha 1 labs" in Figma; page 1 holds batch 1. Brand colours are red, black and white. Headline in a heavy uppercase sans (Poppins since batch 2, AO-03), serif body, black CTA with an arrow, the real wolf and DNA logo. Match batch 1's colours, fonts and spacing, a bit more minimal.
- **AO-02** · 21 and 23 Nov 2026. Colour on type. Headings on a red ground go all white, since black does not read on red. Flat red shapes get texture or pattern, never a bare flat fill. Headlines on dark or black grounds go all white. The red key phrase is for light grounds only: red on black is "not a present-to-eye contrast".
- **AO-03** · 25 Nov 2026, round 3. Headline type: Poppins, one family, one emphasis device.
  - Light grounds: the whole headline in one weight (Poppins ExtraBold), with the red key phrase as the emphasis.
  - Dark grounds: the whole headline white, emphasis by weight (set-up sentence in Poppins Light, pay-off in Poppins ExtraBold). A Light set-up line at about 110 px passed the thumbnail test; step up to Regular if one ever fails.

## Logo

- **AO-04** · 23 and 25 Nov 2026. Always the logo without the "Optimize • Regenerate • Thrive" tagline. On light grounds no card goes behind it: "the logo should do its job itself". It sits on clean, bright ground: if the photo falls off to grey behind it, brighten that part to white. Never over a busy texture such as granite speckle.
- **AO-24** · 26 Nov 2026. On dark grounds the CTA is a white button with an ink label and arrow, matching the reviewed section; never a red button on black. Applied across batch 2 in the round-4 rebuild.
- **AO-05** · 26 Nov 2026, from Shresht's senior (Loom review). On dark grounds (black, graphite, crimson) use the dark-ground version of the logo, with silver-white "LABS", straight on the ground with no card: "I went ahead and made a version of the logo that works on dark backgrounds." It sits in the senior's copy of the file beside the light-ground version. Bring it into the "Alpha 1 labs" file next to the light-ground logo, and use it in place of the white label card.
- **AO-17** · 26 Nov 2026, from Shresht's senior (Loom review). The logo is always a clean copy, never a cut-out: use the senior's clean light-ground and dark-ground versions, brought in from his copy of the file into the "Alpha 1 labs" file. In the Loom the senior zooms in on the batch 2 logo layer ("Logo | Alpha1 Labs (no tagline)", 188 x 81 on 1:1): it is a cut-out with a light matte and shadow left round it, visible on the granite ground. In his words: "you kind of left a bunch of like the white background and stuff like the drop shadow... this looks definitely pretty sloppy. So what you're going to want to do is have some clean copies of the logo." He replaces it with clean copies: chrome-dark "LABS" for light grounds, and a silver-white "LABS" version he made for dark grounds (AO-05). He drops the clean one into the ad at about 270 px wide on 1:1 (up from 188), so it reads at a glance while staying the quiet element (L-07, Q-14): "make them a little bit bigger so that they're actually visible". Never re-cut the logo out of a flattened image, and never use the "(no tagline)" cut-out layer again.

## Product

- **AO-06** · 21 and 23 Nov 2026. Vials carry plain blank white labels, never branded or with added text. A vial is empty or holds fine sand-like powder filling about 10% from the bottom. Put this in every render prompt and check every vial image for a fill line.
- **AO-18** · 26 Nov 2026, from Shresht's senior (Loom review). Vials look like the vials on Alpha One's live website (design-bible.md W-14). The senior imports the site with the html.to.design plugin and scrolls to the product cards. The real Alpha One vial is a short, clear 3 mL multi-dose vial with a red-and-silver flip-off crimp cap; it is shown with a white label naming the product and dose, standing beside its box, with powder inside. The batch 2 renders used a taller jar-like vial with a ribbed red screw cap. He selects it in the dark red-sphere ad: "See how this is a totally different-looking vial with a different-looking cap?" Every render prompt names the crimp-cap vial and puts the site's product photo in as reference, and QC compares the two side by side. Labels stay blank (AO-06): "Obviously, we can't use like the actual labels and everything, but you can still make the vial look similar." Match the shape, glass, cap and proportions, not the printing.

## Direction

- **AO-09** · 21 Nov 2026. Batch 2 review anchors. Liked: the black-ground ad with the red 3D sphere behind the vial, and the full-red ad with the text inside the image. Rejected as too basic: the off-white centred statement layout, and plain white or lab-photo layouts.
- **AO-10** · 26 Nov 2026, from Shresht's senior (Loom review). "Alpha1" in the serif body must read as Alpha-one, never "Alphai": "this 1 is weird, it's like a weird character... make that update kind of across the board." On every text layer that sets "Alpha1" (body, CTA, anywhere), turn on Case-sensitive forms in Figma's type settings; if the font doesn't support it, set Numbers to Lining. QC fails a frame where the 1 still reads like a dotless i (design-bible.md Q-05). This settles the lining-figures question that was waiting in Studio Desk.

## Lessons from Shresht's own edit of ad 01 (23 Nov 2026)

Shresht re-laid ad 01 V1 1x1 by hand and asked for the same changes on the 9:16. Measured against the build, on a 1080 px frame:

- **AO-11** · Headline 80 to 107 px, spanning about 76% of the frame width (820 px). The headline is the dominant mass, not a polite caption.
- **AO-12** · Line break "THE DETAILS SET / THE STANDARD.": near-equal lines forming one solid block, the key phrase kept whole on its own line.
- **AO-13** · Body 32 to 34 px. Gaps between groups opened up: headline to body 24 to 50 px, body to CTA 32 to 88 px, so each group reads on its own.
- **AO-14** · CTA scaled up about 8% and moved onto the white panel. A black CTA reads strongest on white ground.
- **AO-15** · Product scaled 1.56x for presence, its right edge on exactly the same line as the headline's right edge. Product and type share an edge.
- **AO-16** · When V2's copy is longer, shrink its headline so the widest line hits the same measure. Keep every position and the visual identical.

(The 48 px margin from this edit is replaced by design-bible.md Q-02.)

## Lessons from the senior's Loom review (25-26 Nov 2026)

Shresht's senior recorded a 5-minute Loom ("Alpha One Creative Feedback and Logo Fixes"), working in a copy of the Alpha 1 labs file. Its chapters: 00:00 quick feedback overview, 00:55 improve file hygiene, 03:05 fix font and hierarchy, 04:06 match website product look. He worked mostly on RV-B2-MEETALPHA ("THE DETAILS SET THE STANDARD.") in the "ALPHA1 LABS | BATCH #2" section, then the red-sphere "VETERAN-OWNED. DRIVEN BY CURIOSITY." ad. Shresht shared screen recordings of the Loom, and these lessons come from its audio transcript and a frame-by-frame watch. The Loom stops mid-sentence at its 5-minute limit. A layer audit of the "Batch 2 - Ads 08-14" section found the same faults there.

- **AO-19** · Regressions are the worst miss. The 08-14 section shipped with rules already in force broken on every frame. The logo kept its tagline (AO-04). It sat in a white tab on light grounds (AO-04). 1:1 copy started at x 48 instead of 112 (Q-02). Check the build against the rules in force before anything else (design-bible.md W-02 receipt, Q-13 audit). A senior should never have to repeat a note Shresht already gave.
- **AO-20** · The plate under the product was never cleaned. In the Loom the senior drags the MEETALPHA product cut-out (vial + gauge blocks, 494 x 513) aside. Underneath, the background plate (named "product removed under cutout") still shows a ghost of the vial: the red cap smear, the glass outline and a hard cut rectangle. He opens the plate's fill and the residue is right there in the thumbnail. The product could never move without breaking the ad. In the 08-14 section the vial was baked into the plate outright, with the cut-out hidden underneath, and the 9:16s had no product layer at all. In his words: "when you take it off, it's got like all this weird kind of cut-up... this should definitely all be clean and not have any of the weird kind of image treatments... if I wanted to move this around or move the photo around, there's no extra space to do that." He opened a well-built file for another peptide brand to show how it should be: the product as its own image with "nothing weird behind it", and the background "a huge image that I can kind of reposition". Product and a truly empty plate are separate visible layers (design-bible.md Q-13).
- **AO-21** · Leftovers, wrappers and tight crops. He drags the 1:1 and 9:16 plates: they are cropped flush to the frame, and each ratio has its own plate. "These images are the same, like this is all the same asset on both, it's not different assets. And so here you have this one that's just the square, and then over here you have one that's like a different asset... No reason to have that many images for each one." One background asset per ad, placed large in both ratios and repositioned. In the 08-14 section, hidden scrims, contact-shadow ellipses, reflection groups and spare cut-outs were left in most frames, a generator default filename sat on a visible layer, and one 9:16 had two stacked logo tabs. The 9:16 logo sat 290 px down inside a 418 px "Logo tab" frame hanging off the top of the canvas, at a different y on every ad (-95 to -168). One asset, one box, plates with bleed, consistent positions (Q-13).
- **AO-22** · Body hierarchy: a lead line. The MEETALPHA body was three even lines at about 32 px ("Say hello to Alpha1 Labs. / A science-inspired brand shaped / by clarity, curiosity, and care."). The senior split it into two separate text elements: "break this out into two different elements, and then just like really have a little bit more room. Maybe you can like make that bold... I guess there's no bold version for that font. It's fine. You can just make it a little bigger." Result: "now you have a little bit more hierarchy here, and it looks a little bit cleaner". "Say hello to Alpha1 Labs." became a lead line at about 46 px (Sorts Mill Goudy Regular, line height 60). "A science-inspired brand shaped by clarity, curiosity, and care." ran on one full-width line at the smaller body size. Headline, lead line and body now read as three clear steps. He did the same on the 9:16, centred. Apply it wherever a body opens with a short greeting or set-up sentence (design-bible.md L-30).
- **AO-23** · Pull in the website at the start of every batch. In his words: "whenever I start a batch, I usually pull in the website... it's a free plugin, and then you just type in the website, and it'll pull it in." The reason is trust: "whenever a customer clicks on the ad and they're taken to the website, they're not presented with like a totally different product, so that affects like trust-building" (design-bible.md W-14).
