## 28 Nov 2026 - Trend intel

Job T, Saturday deep dive on Behance (Most Appreciated this week and the Advertising gallery). All four projects are makers' concept or spec work; proof is Behance appreciation. Visual references only, never assets to copy. Lasting moves are in trend-intel.md.

- 28 Nov 2026. Aftermath, not action: show the evidence of the problem instead of the problem happening (a ruined pizza box with crime-scene evidence markers, the cat sitting beside it, product pack small in the sign-off corner) (Purina One "Hunger made him do it", Malak Haredy, https://www.behance.net/gallery/258162339/Purina-One-Social-Media-Creative-Campaign). Proof: most appreciated this week, 256 appreciations, 1.8K views. Use it: problem-solution statics where showing the problem directly is ugly or off-limits; works with zero humans, so it fits research-use brands (R-01: the scene after a failed workout, not the person). Supports V-06. The evidence markers are props in the scene, not typographic callouts, so Q-07 holds; keep them wordless.
- 28 Nov 2026. One metaphor, three executions: the campaign's single idea (pet as criminal) runs as three different layout devices, a crime-scene photo, a culprit portrait in a bandana, and a case-file document, with one line and one sign-off across all (same project). Proof: as above. Use it: batch planning, so ads are distinct concepts (V-03) yet read as one campaign (V-01). The case-file execution itself is dense dossier microtype: clashes with Q-07 and V-07, so take the system, not that frame.
- 28 Nov 2026. Miniature crew repairs the problem: macro skin shot where tiny construction workers zip up a crack, plaster and scaffold dry lips, with the jar at real scale in the corner (Vaseline Jelly conceptual ads, art direction Saifullah Sardar, https://www.behance.net/gallery/257809631/Vaseline-Jelly-Poster-DesignConceptual-Ad). Proof: most appreciated this week, 170 appreciations, 2K views. Use it: skincare and supplements where the benefit is "repair" (joints, skin, recovery). Supports V-06 and the depth signal in his taste profile. Its script initial capital inside a sans headline clashes with L-24, and the "Concept Design" corner labels with Q-07: take the scene only.
- 28 Nov 2026. A world built on the body where the product sits: a full basketball arena and crowd sculpted into the ear around the open-ear bud, city skyline behind (HUAWEI FreeClip "Don't shut the world out", multiple owners, https://www.behance.net/gallery/252664371/DONT-SHUT-THE-WORLD-OUT-HUAWEI-FreeClip). Proof: 394 appreciations, 3.3K views; the cover carries an award ribbon ("DA Award 2026") not yet verified. Use it: wearables and earbuds (Solace), any product used on one body part; the product stays large and sharp while the miniature world says the headline. Second sighting of the miniature-world move this week from a different maker.
- 28 Nov 2026. A palette per execution: each key visual in the campaign gets its own named colour harmony (analogous oranges for one, a compound violet and sand for another) while logo, line and type stay fixed (same HUAWEI project). Proof: as above. Use it: own ground per ad inside one batch (V-01) without losing the campaign thread. Supports V-01.
- 28 Nov 2026. The benefit floods the ordinary room: the game's world spills out of the screen onto a teenager's bedroom floor, snow sprays under a snowboard in the room, sand dunes and waves in the other two (Oculus VR "Live the game" key visuals, Re Bo, https://www.behance.net/gallery/256775651/Oculus-VR-Key-Visuals). Proof: most appreciated this week, 362 appreciations, 4.5K views. Use it: any "transport" benefit (sleep, calm, energy): the everyday room stays real and the benefit's world takes over its floor. Supports V-06.
- 28 Nov 2026. Heavy italic lockup on the action's axis: a two-line black-weight italic headline tilted to follow the snowboard's line ("Rule the rush / Live the game"), one family, set in the scene's open sky (same Oculus project). Proof: as above. Use it: sport, energy and performance statics. Supports L-24 and L-28; check L-30 (it must not cross the subject).
- 28 Nov 2026. Texture smear as proof: one clean swipe of the product across a forearm beside the jar, on a flat brand-blue ground, as the calm frame between the busy miniature scenes (same Vaseline project). Proof: as finding 3. Use it: gels, creams, powders: show the texture in use instead of a claim. Q-06 craft floor: the flat ground needs light falloff or texture (L-29 for flat colour).

Use next: a world built on the body for the Solace earbuds (or any wearable), because it is the one move seen twice this week from two makers, the product stays hero-size (V-10) and the scene says the headline (V-06).

## 27 Nov 2026 - Daily layout research

Job A, fallback scan (no new layout candidates in the desk since 24 Nov). Source tested: Ads of the World print statics. Visual references only, never assets to copy; health claims in these ads come from their own briefs, ours only from the claims register.

- 27 Nov 2026. One-hue scene that is the headline's mood: the whole illustrated frame (street, sky, building, figure) sits in one blue because the line is about "the bluest day of the year" (Fybeca vitamin D print "Smile Sunshine", Punto 99, Ecuador 2025: adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/smile-sunshine-a66fc028-4a6f-4512-a607-5254e3a777b1). Why it works: the colour says the problem before a word is read, so the scene carries the headline and the copy can stay small. A sharper case of V-06.
- 27 Nov 2026. Copy set into the scene's open area, small: a three-line headline sits in the open sky over the rooftops at about a tenth of frame height, with no band, card or scrim, while the figure holds the opposite side (Fybeca, same source). Why it works: when the scene does the talking, small type in a naturally quiet area reads as part of the picture, not a label on top (scenario-as-copy). Q-04's thumbnail test still applies.
- 27 Nov 2026. One hand-drawn mark on the turn phrase: the only emphasis is a small hand-drawn sun beside "but only if you allow it", the line where the ad turns from problem to answer, and that line alone steps up to bold (Fybeca, same source). Why it works: the one drawn mark is the product's promise (sunlight, vitamin D) at doodle size, placed exactly where the meaning flips. Confirms hand-drawn marks over icons (Q-07) and one emphasis device (L-25).
- 27 Nov 2026. One-row sign-off lockup: proof line and offer stacked flush right, a thin vertical rule, then the logo, all in one row in the bottom-right corner (Fybeca, same source). Why it works: proof, offer and brand read as one unit in the last place the eye lands, and the rule gives the logo its clear space (L-10).
- 27 Nov 2026. Quote-and-rebut headline: open with a phrase the audience already says, in quote marks, then answer it ("'Rosé all day' doesn't meet your daily nutrient requirement"), centred on one warm ground with the product small at the foot (Nature's Bounty "Complement Yourself", Creative Circus student OOH 2021, not a live ad: adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/complement-yourself). Why it works: borrowing the reader's own line earns the stop and the rebuttal becomes the reason to buy; a short cousin of the letter-to-the-problem seed. Structure only: its script words inside a caps serif break L-24.
- 27 Nov 2026. Try next batch: a one-hue scenario scene for Lucori, a warm everyday moment graded to one honey-cream hue with the Newsreader headline set small in the scene's quiet area, because LU-06 already asks for real everyday scenes and letting the scene carry the line answers V-06 without touching the brand's type system.
