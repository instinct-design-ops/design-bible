## 27 Nov 2026 - LinkedIn to design

From the 8am LinkedIn run. Fictional brand; design lessons only.

- 27 Nov 2026. Sign-panel card for spec lists: a black header bar over ruled rows in one DIN-like grotesk shows a "what's inside" or spec list on a food or supplement static without bullet clutter (signage-utilitarian trend, designrush.com and digitalsynopsis.com 2026 trend round-ups). Why it works: the ruled rows do the job of bullets and read as a real label.
- 27 Nov 2026. Brief card beside the ad it produced, one arrow between: inputs on the left, output on the right. Passed the thumbnail test with a 116 px headline and the product at about 60% of the ad's height. Why it works: the before-and-after is the whole story at a glance.
- 27 Nov 2026. An offer chip never touches the product: the first build had it over the pouch's corner; moved to the bottom-left, the product reads whole. Confirms L-10's clear-space logic for every badge, not only the logo.
- 27 Nov 2026. A blank label on a rendered pack carries the brand as live type, which keeps the pack editable (Q-01, B-07).

## 27 Nov 2026 - Daily layout research

Job A, fallback scan (no new layout candidates in the desk since 24 Nov). Source tested: Ads of the World print statics. Visual references only, never assets to copy; health claims in these ads come from their own briefs, ours only from the claims register.

- 27 Nov 2026. One-hue scene that is the headline's mood: the whole illustrated frame (street, sky, building, figure) sits in one blue because the line is about "the bluest day of the year" (Fybeca vitamin D print "Smile Sunshine", Punto 99, Ecuador 2025: adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/smile-sunshine-a66fc028-4a6f-4512-a607-5254e3a777b1). Why it works: the colour says the problem before a word is read, so the scene carries the headline and the copy can stay small. A sharper case of V-06.
- 27 Nov 2026. Copy set into the scene's open area, small: a three-line headline sits in the open sky over the rooftops at about a tenth of frame height, with no band, card or scrim, while the figure holds the opposite side (Fybeca, same source). Why it works: when the scene does the talking, small type in a naturally quiet area reads as part of the picture, not a label on top (scenario-as-copy). Q-04's thumbnail test still applies.
- 27 Nov 2026. One hand-drawn mark on the turn phrase: the only emphasis is a small hand-drawn sun beside "but only if you allow it", the line where the ad turns from problem to answer, and that line alone steps up to bold (Fybeca, same source). Why it works: the one drawn mark is the product's promise (sunlight, vitamin D) at doodle size, placed exactly where the meaning flips. Confirms hand-drawn marks over icons (Q-07) and one emphasis device (L-25).
- 27 Nov 2026. One-row sign-off lockup: proof line and offer stacked flush right, a thin vertical rule, then the logo, all in one row in the bottom-right corner (Fybeca, same source). Why it works: proof, offer and brand read as one unit in the last place the eye lands, and the rule gives the logo its clear space (L-10).
- 27 Nov 2026. Quote-and-rebut headline: open with a phrase the audience already says, in quote marks, then answer it ("'Rosé all day' doesn't meet your daily nutrient requirement"), centred on one warm ground with the product small at the foot (Nature's Bounty "Complement Yourself", Creative Circus student OOH 2021, not a live ad: adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/complement-yourself). Why it works: borrowing the reader's own line earns the stop and the rebuttal becomes the reason to buy; a short cousin of the letter-to-the-problem seed. Structure only: its script words inside a caps serif break L-24.
- 27 Nov 2026. Try next batch: a one-hue scenario scene for Lucori, a warm everyday moment graded to one honey-cream hue with the Newsreader headline set small in the scene's quiet area, because LU-06 already asks for real everyday scenes and letting the scene carry the line answers V-06 without touching the brand's type system.
