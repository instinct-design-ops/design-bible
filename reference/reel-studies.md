## Reel study: @marc.kaz Plexo promo (research, 23 Nov 2026)

- Studied a 10-second Instagram reel by @marc.kaz (12 Oct 2026, about 6.5k likes) promoting Plexo, a free open-source download manager (github.com/anmolkapil/plexo). Reference for demo-format reels and product-launch statics.
- 23 Nov 2026. Split-screen demo + reactor: product UI (the app's dark dashboard) on the top half, creator face-cam on the bottom half, pointing up at the screen. The pointing gesture aims the eye at the demo; the face supplies the trust.
- 23 Nov 2026. One fixed banner claim for the whole reel: emoji + all-caps benefit headline ('MERGE EVERY INTERNET CONNECTION INTO ONE DOWNLOAD') pinned to the top of the frame. One idea, never leaves the screen.
- 23 Nov 2026. A live counter is the proof: the download speed visibly climbs from 2 to 51 MB/s as the connections merge - a self-playing before/after that needs no copy. When a product has a number that moves, put the number on screen and let it run.
- 23 Nov 2026. The conversion path never disappears: the GitHub URL sits as a persistent overlay at the bottom of every frame, and the caption repeats it as a spec sheet (feature bullets, platforms, license, star count). The reel hooks, the caption closes.

## Reel study: @joshuaseage Couch TV editor teaser (research, 23 Nov 2026)

- What it is: a 36-second pre-launch teaser for his own Couch TV editor - a web editor for custom TV home screens plus a companion app ('pick one of my designs or design your own TV... as easy as moving apps around on your phone screen'). Same creator as the TV home-screen carousel study above; this reel announces the tool behind those designs. Source: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DgEL9mOmEAE/ (22 Nov 2026).
- Sneak peek as the whole format: the reel's only job is the tease ('Here is your sneak peek at the new Couch TV editor') and the only ask is a follow ('Follow me for the launch', repeated in the video and twice in the caption). Pre-launch content sells anticipation and asks for the smallest possible commitment.
- Demo-as-background retention: he talks to camera while the monitor behind him cycles to a new home-screen layout about every two seconds. The talking head carries the story; the changing screen supplies the visual variety that holds attention and doubles as proof the product is real.
- Position against the alternative by name: 'You don't need AI' frames the current way (AI generation) as friction, then the pitch lands on a familiarity metaphor: 'as easy as moving apps around on your phone screen'. Name what you replace, then claim simplicity in terms the buyer already knows.
- Burned word-by-word captions with a blue keyword highlight mark the load-bearing words (sneak peek, editor, You don't need AI, home screen) - the emphasis tells a muted viewer exactly what matters.
- Build-in-public loop: the account's design content (the TV home-screen carousels) feeds the product launch; the audience built on the content becomes the launch list.

## Reel study: @navio.io iPhone-Duo hinge animation app (Shresht send, 25 Nov 2026)

- Silent 16-second product demo, 4.4K likes / 1.3K comments. One static phone shot of a MacBook in a kitchen, one overlay line ("iPhone Duo style hinge animation on MacBook"), a hand closes the lid and the desktop frosts and folds away with the lid angle, unfolding on open. No face, no voiceover, no edits - the effect is the hook. Lessons: (1) one astonishing visual beats any benefit stack - 16s of pure demo; (2) newsjack a hot launch then one-up it: caption opens "Apple revealed the iPhone Duo two days ago. It's already running on MacBooks"; (3) video shows magic, caption carries the mechanism ("newer MacBooks already have a lid-angle sensor") - credibility lives in the caption; (4) comment-to-DM funnel: "Comment 'Fold' to get the link in your DM" drove ~1 comment per 3.4 likes - the bait boosts reach AND captures leads. Static-ad translation: one impossible-looking image + one-line overlay, mechanism in the caption, link gated behind a comment.

## Reel study: @danteasemmm 7 motion skills for Claude Code (Shresht send, 25 Nov 2026)

- 48s listicle, 20K likes / 1.2K comments. Seven tools that let Claude Code animate anything you build: HyperFrames (plain HTML to finished video), Emil Kowalski's animation skills, DesignBookmark Motion (copies a site's motion into Claude/Codex), Remotion skills (videos in React), Blender Skills, Motion Transitions + springs, Motion Canvas (code-driven explainers). Format lessons: numbered demo cards with a "1 of 5" pager UI carry the reel while the speaker stays small in the corner - let the demos be the content; same comment-to-DM CTA ("comment 'Duh'", joke price "free.99"); caption rides the same iPhone Duo hashtag trend for reach even though the content is a skills list. Actionable for us: HyperFrames / Remotion / DesignBookmark Motion plug into Claude Code for animated product demos and motion ads.

## Reel study: @aiwithkaushall Claude artifacts as desktop apps (Shresht send, 25 Nov 2026)

- Kaushal Pandey (@aiwithkaushall) covers a same-day ClaudeDevs announcement: Claude artifacts can now be installed as desktop apps on Mac and Windows. Workflow: ask Claude to build an artifact, click "Install as app", and it opens like any other app - installed artifacts can use your connectors and send notifications, so a Claude-built tool (dashboard, tracker, mini-app) becomes a permanent desktop utility instead of a chat tab. Format note for the AI-news reel genre: the source receipt (the ClaudeDevs post) stays pinned at the top of frame for the entire reel while the creator talks and screen-demo B-roll plays behind him - the claim is verifiable on screen the whole time, which is what makes this content feel trustworthy.
